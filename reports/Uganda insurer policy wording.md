# Build Uganda’s Representative Policy Test Pack

Uganda does not have one defensible “average insurer” catalogue or a universal policy wording. IRA’s July 2026 release reported 2025 gross written premiums of UGX 977.6 billion for life, representing 48.31% of the market, with non-life at 49.49%; IRA’s annual-report page lists the 2025 market report as a draft extract. The latest product-class ranking located is UIA’s 2024 list: individual life, medical, comprehensive motor, fire, deposit-administered schemes and credit life. The near-equal segment shares and product-class ranking offer a practical test starting point, not a claim that a typical insurer sells every class. For onboarding tests, start with a synthetic comprehensive motor bundle, then cover the leading life, health, property/fire and credit-life classes, with motor third-party separate because its protection differs materially. A useful test bundle links the schedule, base wording, claim steps, evidence requests and any selected endorsements while preserving where each extracted rule came from. IIP’s existing wording flow proposes claims-related configuration for human review; it does not set up every product term. The prompt included below produces actual fictional documents for that workflow, with a clean baseline and controlled mismatch variants.

## Market premium data points to six high-value test classes

IRA’s July 2026 announcement says 2025 life gross written premiums reached UGX 977.6 billion, or 48.31% of total market premiums, with non-life representing 49.49%. IRA’s annual-report page currently labels the 2025 market report a “Draft 2025 Market Report Extract,” so the aggregate figures should be treated as published preliminary reporting rather than as a final annual report. The latest product-class ranking located remains the UIA 2024 report: individual life (UGX 379.22 billion), medical (UGX 354.92 billion), motor comprehensive (UGX 191.23 billion), fire (UGX 174.05 billion), deposit-administered schemes (UGX 98.26 billion) and credit life (UGX 81.87 billion). UIA labels these class statistics unaudited and attributes them to IRA Q4 2024 figures. The class ranking is a prioritization proxy; the reviewed sources do not show a current public insurer-by-insurer median product menu. ([IRA 2025 performance announcement](https://ira.go.ug/insurance-premiums-cross-ugx-2-trillion-as-life-business-sparks-historic-growth/), [IRA annual reports page](https://www.ira.go.ug/annual/), [UIA 2024 Integrated Report](https://uia.co.ug/sites/default/files/2025-06/UIA%20Annual%20Report%202024.pdf))

The Insurance Regulatory Authority’s 2024 market report and insurer catalogues show a wider market: life assurance, group life, group credit, health, personal accident, marine, liability, engineering, agriculture and other classes are also present. Uganda’s Insurance Act likewise recognizes a broad set of life and non-life classes. For IIP, the six leading premium classes make a sensible first test corpus; personal accident and group life are the next useful additions, while marine and engineering test more specialized documents. This is a prioritization inference from market reporting and public product catalogs, not a claim that every insurer offers these products. ([IRA 2024 Annual Insurance Market Report](https://ira.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2024-Insurance-Industry-market-report.pdf), [Insurance Act, Cap. 213](https://www.businesslicences.go.ug/uploads/documents/theinsuranceact-1.pdf), [ICEA LION Uganda](https://icealion.co.ug/))

## The bundle must connect schedules, wording and endorsements

Public examples show why onboarding should treat a policy as related documents rather than one flat text. A schedule can identify insured parties, subject matter, period, limits and selected endorsements; the base wording supplies definitions, cover, exclusions, conditions and claims procedures; a proposal can contain declarations; and an endorsement can change a particular term. In a published fire wording, extensions depend on being referenced in the schedule, so the system should retain the link between the schedule selection and the endorsement itself. No source reviewed establishes a single mandatory Uganda-wide schedule layout or field schema. ([Jubilee Allianz General Fire Insurance Policy](https://www.beanafrica.com/Allianz/ug/PolicyDocs/Fire%20Policy.pdf), [Insurance Appeals Tribunal, UAP Old Mutual v African Queen Ltd](https://iat.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Application-01-of-2022-UAP-Old-Mutual-Insurance-Uganda-Ltd-vs-African-Queen-Limited.pdf), [UIA Traders’ Insurance Solutions Booklet](https://www.uia.co.ug/sites/default/files/2024-12/Traders%E2%80%99%20Insurance%20Solutions%20Booklet%20ENGLISH.pdf))

The exact fields differ by product. Motor needs vehicle identifiers, use, cover sections, insured value, excess and third-party limits. Fire/property needs the location and type of property, sums insured, covered perils and any selected extensions. Life and credit life need the insured person or borrower, beneficiary or lender, benefit, term and premium details. Health needs members, benefit limits, waiting periods and network or authorization terms. These are useful extraction targets inferred from published products and forms, not a regulator-prescribed universal schema. The policy and schedule generated for each test must control what IIP is expected to extract.

Claim evidence also varies by event and policy. IRA motor guidance and claims guidelines refer to evidence such as a claim form, driving permit, police report or sketch, logbook and third-party demand where applicable; IRA life guidance describes death-claim evidence such as a claim form, NIRA death certificate, policy document and beneficiary details. These examples should inform synthetic documents, but they must not be presented as universal requirements: IIP should propose a required document only when the wording or insurer-approved rule supports it. ([IRA Insurance Claims Guidelines](https://ira.go.ug/cp/uploads/claimesguidelines.pdf), [IRA, Endowment Policy Claims](https://ira.go.ug/when-to-place-a-claim-on-that-endowment-insurance-policy))

## IIP’s current onboarding flow favors short, cited claim rules

The current implementation extracts candidate claim types, required claim documents, claim questions, covered items and exclusions from a stored policy wording. It attaches source quotes and clause references for review; staff can accept, edit or reject proposals before adding accepted items to a new draft product version. This is a claims-process configuration workflow, not a complete product setup: it does not derive all product identity, pricing, limits, underwriting rules or contract approvals from the document. Keep the synthetic prompt focused on readable, evidence-backed claims rules and reviewable proposals. (Repository references: `src/InsuranceIntelligence.Application/Products/WordingExtraction.cs`, `src/InsuranceIntelligence.Application/Products/WordingDraftService.cs`, and `docs/build-loop/BUILD_PLAN.md`.)

For reliable extraction, include explicit “Page N” markers, stable clause numbering and distinctive sentences that can be quoted verbatim. Keep the baseline under 60,000 characters. Claim-document keys must match a built-in or active workspace document type; the current built-in examples include `ClaimForm`, `PoliceAbstract`, `Invoice`, `MedicalReport`, `PhotoEvidence`, `CustomerId` and `GarageEstimate`. If a test needs a logbook or driving permit as its own document type, first add that custom type to the workspace catalog. The parser recognizes `Select` questions, but the current review panel does not expose that field type, so the prompt uses text, date, number and yes/no questions for the baseline. (Repository references: `src/InsuranceIntelligence.Domain/Documents/DocumentTypeDefinition.cs`, `src/InsuranceIntelligence.Application/Products/WordingExtraction.cs`, and `web/portal/components/iip/WordingDraftPanel.tsx`.)

## Use generated documents as QA fixtures only

The Insurance Act provides for approval of policy and proposal text and format before issue and approval for amendments. Generated text should therefore be conspicuously synthetic and must not be described as approved, legally sufficient or ready for sale. The sources do not establish a national average premium, excess, limit or standard clause; test figures should be arbitrary and internally consistent, not labeled as market averages. ([Insurance Act, sections 64–65](https://ira.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Final-Insurance-Act-1.pdf))

Use the reusable [Claude policy-wording test prompt](../docs/build-loop/POLICY_WORDING_TEST_PROMPT.md) to generate a clean baseline bundle and separate variants. Start with comprehensive motor because it exercises the broadest set of linked schedule fields, coverage, exclusions, claim categories and evidence types. Then generate separate life, health and property/fire examples rather than forcing one template across them. The private answer key should map each expected extraction to its document, page or clause and verbatim evidence; mismatches should be called data inconsistencies for human review, never treated as proof of fraud.

## Conclusion

The strongest onboarding test is not the longest document: it is a compact, internally consistent bundle that tests whether IIP extracts a claim rule, ties it to exact evidence, and leaves uncertainty visible. A deliberately inconsistent companion bundle then tests whether the reviewer can see and resolve conflicts without the system inventing missing terms or making a consequential decision. Use current insurer-approved materials before configuring any real tenant product.
