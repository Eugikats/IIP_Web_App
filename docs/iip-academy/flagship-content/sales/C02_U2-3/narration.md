### SLIDE 02 - Prepare a useful handover

**On-screen text:** Include the stated need, relevant facts, source documents, unanswered questions, contact status and... · A transcript can support the record but is not a substitute for a...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Include the stated need, relevant facts, source documents, unanswered questions, contact status and next action. Distinguish an actual quote or issued policy from a draft. A transcript can support the record but is not a substitute for a concise explanation of what the receiving person needs to do.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Transfer ownership explicitly

**On-screen text:** Name the receiving person or queue, the action and the expected review point... · Avoid telling a customer an officer will call at a time no one...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Name the receiving person or queue, the action and the expected review point under the team's procedure. Sending a note does not prove the other team accepted ownership. Record acknowledgement or retain responsibility for checking that the handover arrived. Avoid telling a customer an officer will call at a time no one has accepted.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Keep channels consistent

**On-screen text:** Connect the handover to the relevant enquiry or policy reference without exposing restricted... · Check the recipient and permitted channel each time information crosses a team boundary.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Connect the handover to the relevant enquiry or policy reference without exposing restricted information. An agent and claims officer may need different views of the same relationship. Do not copy an entire internal conversation into a customer message. Check the recipient and permitted channel each time information crosses a team boundary.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Follow up on the next action

**On-screen text:** Follow-up asks whether the agreed next action happened and what has changed. · Close or pause stale work with a reason instead of leaving it as...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Follow-up asks whether the agreed next action happened and what has changed. It is not simply another sales message. Check for a reply, a stop request, an outstanding service issue or a completed application before preparing a draft. Close or pause stale work with a reason instead of leaving it as an apparently active opportunity indefinitely.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A branch sends an enquiry to an insurer team. The record says 'sent' but no owner has accepted it. The customer returns to the branch and is told to repeat the same...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A branch sends an enquiry to an insurer team. The record says 'sent' but no owner has accepted it. The customer returns to the branch and is told to repeat the same information. A better process keeps the branch responsible for confirming handover acceptance, supplies the existing facts and tells the customer the confirmed next step rather than an invented callback promise.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Write the sending team's handover and the receiving team's acknowledgement. Include how unresolved ownership will be escalated and what the customer can be told now.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Write the sending team's handover and the receiving team's acknowledgement. Include how unresolved ownership will be escalated and what the customer can be told now. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
