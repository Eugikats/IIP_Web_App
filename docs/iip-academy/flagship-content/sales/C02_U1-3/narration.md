### SLIDE 02 - Find the applicable source

**On-screen text:** Check the insurer, product name, source owner, version and effective period before drafting. · If sources conflict, mark the conflict and ask the authorised owner; do not...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Check the insurer, product name, source owner, version and effective period before drafting. Two similarly named products can have different terms. A marketing summary may omit conditions that appear in the wording. If sources conflict, mark the conflict and ask the authorised owner; do not combine whichever sentences make the offer sound strongest.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Link claims to passages

**On-screen text:** For each statement about a benefit, exclusion, condition or premium, identify the supporting... · AI-generated citations are suggestions until opened and checked.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
For each statement about a benefit, exclusion, condition or premium, identify the supporting passage in the applicable approved source. A link to an entire folder is less useful than a specific document and passage. If support is absent, omit the claim or turn it into a referral question. AI-generated citations are suggestions until opened and checked.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Simplify without changing meaning

**On-screen text:** Use short sentences and explain unfamiliar terms, but retain qualifications. · Present material limitations next to the relevant benefit so the customer does not...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Use short sentences and explain unfamiliar terms, but retain qualifications. 'Subject to assessment' must not become 'will be paid'. A deductible or excess must not disappear from a benefit explanation. Present material limitations next to the relevant benefit so the customer does not receive an attractive headline and miss the condition elsewhere.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Review for the actual question

**On-screen text:** Read the draft as the customer would. · Clarity does not replace the insurer's acceptance, pricing or issuance process.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Read the draft as the customer would. Does it answer their question, name what is unresolved and state a useful next step? Then compare it with the source sentence by sentence. A person authorised for the task confirms advice or a formal quote. Clarity does not replace the insurer's acceptance, pricing or issuance process.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A fictional approved training extract says accidental damage is considered subject to the policy terms, and an excess applies. A draft says, 'Any damage will be paid in full.' It changes both...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A fictional approved training extract says accidental damage is considered subject to the policy terms, and an excess applies. A draft says, 'Any damage will be paid in full.' It changes both the scope and the amount. Correct it to preserve the conditions and excess, then refer unanswered details. The customer-facing explanation should not invent a figure because the training extract does not supply one.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Build a three-column evidence table: proposed statement, exact supporting passage, and review action. Include one benefit, one limitation and one unresolved question.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Build a three-column evidence table: proposed statement, exact supporting passage, and review action. Include one benefit, one limitation and one unresolved question. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
