### SLIDE 02 - Start with verified policy information

**On-screen text:** Check the applicable policy status, expiry date, version and owner. · When a date or status is uncertain, resolve it before a customer-facing statement.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Check the applicable policy status, expiry date, version and owner. A reminder worklist is only as reliable as its source. Do not infer that a policy renewed because a message was sent or a payment was attempted. When a date or status is uncertain, resolve it before a customer-facing statement.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Review changed needs

**On-screen text:** Ask whether the relevant circumstances have changed and capture the customer's own answer. · An appropriate renewal conversation can also reveal that the customer no longer wants...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Ask whether the relevant circumstances have changed and capture the customer's own answer. A changed vehicle use, address or business activity may require referral. Do not assume last year's facts remain true. An appropriate renewal conversation can also reveal that the customer no longer wants the product or needs service before considering another offer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Keep renewal separate from a new sale

**On-screen text:** An additional product needs its own explanation, applicable source and authorised process. · Avoid duplicate offers for cover already held.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An additional product needs its own explanation, applicable source and authorised process. Existing-customer status is not automatic permission for every campaign. Check current contact status and distinguish necessary policy service from optional marketing under the organisation's procedure. Avoid duplicate offers for cover already held.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Close the loop accurately

**On-screen text:** Record the actual result: needs clarification, referred, not proceeding, awaiting insurer action or... · A claim outcome alone does not determine the renewal outcome.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Record the actual result: needs clarification, referred, not proceeding, awaiting insurer action or confirmed renewed policy. Do not label an invitation as a renewal. Link the new policy version or confirmed outcome where available, preserve history and assign any remaining action. A claim outcome alone does not determine the renewal outcome.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

A policyholder's vehicle use changed from private travel to deliveries. An automated reminder assumes the same terms and says renewal is complete. Correct the unsupported completion statement, capture the changed use and refer through the insurer's process. The customer should receive the actual confirmed terms and effective dates only when established.

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Create a renewal review checklist and two messages: one inviting a review and one communicating a genuinely confirmed outcome. List the evidence required before the second can be sent.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Create a renewal review checklist and two messages: one inviting a review and one communicating a genuinely confirmed outcome. List the evidence required before the second can be sent. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
