**On-screen text:** Understand the task · Work through a fictional case · Practise and check your reasoning

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Welcome to Review points and stop conditions. The learning goal is to specify meaningful human review, authority and stopping rules for a customer-facing workflow. We will connect the method to a fictional insurance scenario, then ask you to prepare a work product you can check. The examples are not insurer product advice and do not establish that any feature is live.

### SLIDE 02 - Define the action boundary

**On-screen text:** Describe exactly what the workflow may read, prepare and change. · Explicitly exclude policy issuance, binding premiums, adverse claim decisions and payment authorisation from...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Describe exactly what the workflow may read, prepare and change. 'Help with sales' is too broad. 'Prepare a reply from an approved source for an authorised officer to review' is testable. Explicitly exclude policy issuance, binding premiums, adverse claim decisions and payment authorisation from the assistant's powers.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Make review possible

**On-screen text:** Show the draft, supporting source, customer context, unknowns and intended recipient to the... · Do not assume a signature field makes a review meaningful if the person...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Show the draft, supporting source, customer context, unknowns and intended recipient to the reviewer. Give the person a real choice to edit, return, stop or refer. Do not assume a signature field makes a review meaningful if the person cannot inspect the evidence or is expected to approve too many items to check.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Specify stop and escalation triggers

**On-screen text:** Missing or conflicting sources, an unmatched customer, a stop request, a complaint and... · A failure should not silently switch to an unapproved tool, channel or source...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Missing or conflicting sources, an unmatched customer, a stop request, a complaint and an unsupported consequential statement require an appropriate pause or route. Define which actions stop and who investigates. A failure should not silently switch to an unapproved tool, channel or source just to make the workflow appear successful.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Record and test the control

**On-screen text:** Keep the draft version, reviewer action and actual result traceable without unnecessarily exposing... · An instruction written in a prompt is not proof that the system enforces...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Keep the draft version, reviewer action and actual result traceable without unnecessarily exposing sensitive data. Test stop conditions with fictional cases before a wider pilot. Verify that a queued message does not go out after a relevant stop request. An instruction written in a prompt is not proof that the system enforces it.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A customer withdraws outreach permission after a follow-up draft has been prepared. The workflow continues because it checked contact status only when the lead was first created. The corrected design rechecks the...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A customer withdraws outreach permission after a follow-up draft has been prepared. The workflow continues because it checked contact status only when the lead was first created. The corrected design rechecks the relevant status before approval/send, stops the queued action and assigns any necessary service work separately. Test this sequence rather than relying on a label saying 'human in the loop'.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Map one workflow with permitted inputs, preparation steps, reviewer evidence, approved action, stop triggers, owner and test case for each trigger.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Map one workflow with permitted inputs, preparation steps, reviewer evidence, approved action, stop triggers, owner and test case for each trigger. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
