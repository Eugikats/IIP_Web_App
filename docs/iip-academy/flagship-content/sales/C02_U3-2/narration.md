### SLIDE 02 - Start with the customer's concern

**On-screen text:** Acknowledge the issue in clear, respectful language without making an admission or promise... · The immediate purpose is to understand the concern and provide a useful, supported...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Acknowledge the issue in clear, respectful language without making an admission or promise outside your role. Avoid blaming a colleague, the customer or an insurer partner. The immediate purpose is to understand the concern and provide a useful, supported next step, not to defend an automated reply.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Separate status from expectation

**On-screen text:** A recorded request for documents is a status. · Where a date is not established, do not invent one to make the...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A recorded request for documents is a status. 'Payment will arrive tomorrow' is an outcome promise requiring evidence and authority. State what is confirmed, what remains outstanding and who is acting. Where a date is not established, do not invent one to make the message sound reassuring.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Route complaints deliberately

**On-screen text:** Identify an expression of dissatisfaction and use the organisation's approved complaints process. · The responsible team owns final complaint responses and any formal dispute communication.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Identify an expression of dissatisfaction and use the organisation's approved complaints process. Record the substance, relevant history and action required. A customer asking for a person should not be trapped in repeated automated answers. The responsible team owns final complaint responses and any formal dispute communication.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Review tone and disclosure

**On-screen text:** Remove internal blame, speculation, fraud suspicions and restricted notes from customer messages. · Keep an accurate record of what was authorised and communicated.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Remove internal blame, speculation, fraud suspicions and restricted notes from customer messages. Check the recipient and source, then read the message for clarity and empathy. Preserve the actual insurer decision rather than softening it into a different promise. Keep an accurate record of what was authorised and communicated.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A customer says, 'I have sent the document twice and nobody tells me what is happening.' The record confirms one upload and an unresolved review task. Do not claim that the file...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A customer says, 'I have sent the document twice and nobody tells me what is happening.' The record confirms one upload and an unresolved review task. Do not claim that the file is complete or payment approved. Acknowledge the concern, state the verified position and route the upload discrepancy and service concern to a named owner. Give only a confirmed next step.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Draft a service update, an internal handover and a complaint-routing note for the same situation. Explain which facts belong in each and which internal details must remain restricted.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Draft a service update, an internal handover and a complaint-routing note for the same situation. Explain which facts belong in each and which internal details must remain restricted. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
