# C02_U3-1: Handling questions and uncertainty

Authored teaching draft. Fictional scenarios, not actual policy advice. Source and expert review remain part of release checks.

### SLIDE 01 - Handling questions and uncertainty

**On-screen text:** Understand the task · Work through a fictional case · Practise and check your reasoning

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Welcome to Handling questions and uncertainty. The learning goal is to give a useful answer from evidence or refer with context when the answer is not established. We will connect the method to a fictional insurance scenario, then ask you to prepare a work product you can check. The examples are not insurer product advice and do not establish that any feature is live.

### SLIDE 02 - Classify the question

**On-screen text:** Distinguish a general product question from an account-specific request, a complaint or a... · A loss report should enter claims intake rather than a marketing discovery form.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Distinguish a general product question from an account-specific request, a complaint or a loss report. The information needed and disclosure checks differ. A general explanation should not expose policyholder details. A loss report should enter claims intake rather than a marketing discovery form.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - State what the evidence supports

**On-screen text:** Check the applicable source and the customer's question before answering. · A clear 'not yet confirmed' with a next action is more useful than...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Check the applicable source and the customer's question before answering. Separate established facts from what is pending. If the source supports only a general explanation, do not infer the customer's own coverage. A clear 'not yet confirmed' with a next action is more useful than a confident unsupported answer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Escalate with a question

**On-screen text:** A useful referral states the exact uncertainty, what was checked and what the... · Avoid promising a response time that has not been accepted.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A useful referral states the exact uncertainty, what was checked and what the receiving person needs to resolve. Do not send a vague 'please assist' without context. Urgency should reflect the actual situation and agreed procedure, not the drafting tool's guess. Avoid promising a response time that has not been accepted.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Check the answer reached the need

**On-screen text:** When a response returns, verify that it answers the original question and is... · If the customer remains confused, clarify or provide a person rather than endlessly...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
When a response returns, verify that it answers the original question and is appropriate for the recipient. Do not forward an internal note unchanged. Record the source of any confirmed answer and the next action. If the customer remains confused, clarify or provide a person rather than endlessly repeating the same automated wording.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A customer asks whether an accident involving delivery work is covered. The available document is a product overview rather than the customer's policy. Explain that the overview cannot establish the claim outcome,...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A customer asks whether an accident involving delivery work is covered. The available document is a product overview rather than the customer's policy. Explain that the overview cannot establish the claim outcome, capture the question and route it to the authorised team with the relevant policy and incident checks still needed. Do not equate a general benefit description with a decision on this event.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Prepare an answer-or-escalate record with the question, sources checked, supported facts, unresolved issue, owner and customer-facing next step.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Prepare an answer-or-escalate record with the question, sources checked, supported facts, unresolved issue, owner and customer-facing next step. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
