# C02_U4-3: Designing a small practical pilot

Authored teaching draft. Fictional scenarios, not actual policy advice. Source and expert review remain part of release checks.

### SLIDE 01 - Designing a small practical pilot

**On-screen text:** Understand the task · Work through a fictional case · Practise and check your reasoning

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Welcome to Designing a small practical pilot. The learning goal is to prepare a bounded pilot that tests usefulness, review controls and customer outcomes before expansion. We will connect the method to a fictional insurance scenario, then ask you to prepare a work product you can check. The examples are not insurer product advice and do not establish that any feature is live.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 02 - Choose one bounded problem

**On-screen text:** Start with a task the team understands and can measure, such as preparing... · Do not begin with a broad promise to automate sales or select a...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Start with a task the team understands and can measure, such as preparing complete enquiry handovers for one approved product. Define the current problem, baseline and affected people. Do not begin with a broad promise to automate sales or select a tool before deciding what useful work it should perform.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Set scope and responsibilities

**On-screen text:** Name the owner, reviewers, users, source documents, allowed information and channels. · Keep the pilot separate from an unreviewed live campaign.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Name the owner, reviewers, users, source documents, allowed information and channels. Specify what remains manual and which decisions the pilot must never make. Use fictional material for initial tests and follow the organisation's approval process for any real information. Keep the pilot separate from an unreviewed live campaign.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Test the hard cases

**On-screen text:** Include ordinary enquiries as well as missing information, conflicting product sources, stop requests,... · Recheck the relevant sequence after a change; do not count an untested edit...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Include ordinary enquiries as well as missing information, conflicting product sources, stop requests, wrong recipients and requests for a person. Define expected behaviour before running the test. Log actual results and correct failures. Recheck the relevant sequence after a change; do not count an untested edit as a successful control.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Decide and document the next step

**On-screen text:** Review quality, customer outcomes, workload and commercial measures together. · Expansion requires a fresh check of sources, ownership, capacity and controls at the...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review quality, customer outcomes, workload and commercial measures together. Recommend continue, change or stop with supporting evidence. State sample limits and outstanding issues. A successful small test does not prove every product or channel is ready. Expansion requires a fresh check of sources, ownership, capacity and controls at the intended scale.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A branch pilots AI-prepared handovers for a single fictional training product. The normal example is complete, but a conflicting-source case still produces a confident benefit claim. The pilot should not be declared...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A branch pilots AI-prepared handovers for a single fictional training product. The normal example is complete, but a conflicting-source case still produces a confident benefit claim. The pilot should not be declared successful because most outputs looked good. Correct the source-conflict route, repeat that case and review the broader test set before requesting any live expansion.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Prepare a one-page pilot plan with problem, scope, sources, roles, boundaries, test cases, metrics, stop conditions, review date and a continue/change/stop recommendation template.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Prepare a one-page pilot plan with problem, scope, sources, roles, boundaries, test cases, metrics, stop conditions, review date and a continue/change/stop recommendation template. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION
