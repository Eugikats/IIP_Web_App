# C02_U1-2: Discovery without guessing

Authored teaching draft. Fictional scenarios, not actual policy advice. Source and expert review remain part of release checks.

### SLIDE 01 - Discovery without guessing

**On-screen text:** Understand the task · Work through a fictional case · Practise and check your reasoning

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Welcome to Discovery without guessing. The learning goal is to collect a useful needs record while keeping assumptions and unnecessary personal information out. We will connect the method to a fictional insurance scenario, then ask you to prepare a work product you can check. The examples are not insurer product advice and do not establish that any feature is live.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 02 - Begin with purpose

**On-screen text:** Start by asking what the customer wants to understand or protect. · Discovery should make the next conversation easier, not collect every detail that might...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Start by asking what the customer wants to understand or protect. A broad question lets the person describe the need in their own words. Do not begin with a long form or a product recommendation. Explain why a follow-up fact is relevant before asking for it. Discovery should make the next conversation easier, not collect every detail that might someday be useful.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - Ask in layers

**On-screen text:** Use an open question, a focused clarification and a short confirmation. · Ask one clear question at a time when using messaging.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Use an open question, a focused clarification and a short confirmation. For a business-owner enquiry, first ask what concern prompted the conversation, then clarify the activity relevant to that concern. Summarise the answer back for correction. Avoid leading questions that suggest a preferred response. Ask one clear question at a time when using messaging.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Label the evidence

**On-screen text:** A useful record distinguishes a customer statement, a verified document fact, an assumption... · Include when the statement was made and the source of a verified fact.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A useful record distinguishes a customer statement, a verified document fact, an assumption and an unknown. If a customer says they have existing cover, record it as stated until the applicable policy is checked. Do not silently promote a conversational answer into a verified product or eligibility fact. Include when the statement was made and the source of a verified fact.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - Know when to refer

**On-screen text:** Collect only information required for the immediate task under the organisation's approved process. · Do not place identity documents, financial details or health information into an unapproved...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Collect only information required for the immediate task under the organisation's approved process. A customer may not know the answer or may prefer to discuss it with an officer. Record the gap and refer instead of guessing or pressuring. Do not place identity documents, financial details or health information into an unapproved AI tool simply to make the summary richer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through the fictional case

**On-screen text:** A shop owner says, 'I need insurance for my business.' The assistant assumes the shop owns the building and drafts a building-cover explanation. The customer actually rents a stall and is worried...

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A shop owner says, 'I need insurance for my business.' The assistant assumes the shop owns the building and drafts a building-cover explanation. The customer actually rents a stall and is worried about stock in transit. Correct the record by removing the unsupported ownership assumption. Ask which loss concerns them, what activity is involved and whether they want an officer to explain relevant options. The next officer receives the stated concern and the unanswered questions, not an invented business profile.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Prepare the practical work

**On-screen text:** Create five discovery questions for the shop owner. For each, state its purpose and how the answer changes the next action. Then write a record with known facts, stated-but-unverified facts and unknowns.

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Create five discovery questions for the shop owner. For each, state its purpose and how the answer changes the next action. Then write a record with known facts, stated-but-unverified facts and unknowns. Pause the lesson and create the work in your own words. Use fictional information. Where a source or fact is missing, mark it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumption. Keep your first draft so that you can compare it with the checked version.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 08 - Review your work

**On-screen text:** Evidence · Meaning · Recipient · Authority · Next action

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Review your work in five passes. First, check each factual statement against the information supplied. Second, check that simplified wording has not changed the meaning. Third, check the intended recipient and whether the information is appropriate for them. Fourth, identify the authority required for any consequential action. Finally, make sure the next step has an owner and that unresolved questions remain visible. Record a correction you made and why it matters to the customer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 09 - Compare an unsafe and a useful response

**On-screen text:** Unsupported certainty hides a gap · A checked next step makes the gap manageable

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An unsafe work product can look complete because it substitutes certainty for missing evidence. A useful one says what is known, what remains unconfirmed and what an authorised person should do next. Apply that distinction to your own draft. If you have written that a policy is active, a price is final, a claim will be paid or an improvement is proven, identify the record that supports that statement. Remove or refer any conclusion that the source does not establish.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 10 - Your takeaways and independent quiz

**On-screen text:** Explain the method · Complete the task · Take the ten-question quiz independently

**Visual:** Editable teaching content; emphasise the method or fictional case rather than a fabricated system screenshot.

NARRATION:
The core outcome is to collect a useful needs record while keeping assumptions and unnecessary personal information out. Keep your completed practice work and the reasons for your corrections. Use the independent ten-question quiz to check your understanding. The unit is part of a broader course, so connect this method to the enquiry, service and review responsibilities you learned earlier. The assistant must not complete your assessment for you.
END NARRATION
