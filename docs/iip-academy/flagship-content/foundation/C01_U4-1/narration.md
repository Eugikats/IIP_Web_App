### SLIDE 03 - Core concepts and terminology

**On-screen text:** Fairness and bias.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Fairness and bias. Models learn from historical data, which may reflect past unfair practices or under-representation of some groups. Proxy discrimination occurs when a variable that seems neutral : a location, a phone type, a mobile-money pattern : closely tracks a characteristic that should not drive decisions. Explainability. Customers declined, loaded or delayed are entitled to understand why; complex models can make this hard, and the IAIS treats transparency and explainability as a core supervisory topic. Robustness and security. Models can fail on unusual inputs, degrade as conditions change (model drift), be manipulated (prompt injection, Unit 3.3), or leak sensitive data. Third-party risk. Most insurers will use vendor models and cloud platforms; the insurer remains accountable for outcomes, so contracts, data terms, audit rights and exit plans matter.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Core concepts and terminology

**On-screen text:** Conduct and customer outcomes.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Conduct and customer outcomes. AI that pushes products customers do not need, misstates cover, or makes claims harder to pursue creates conduct risk. Over-reliance. Staff may lose skills or stop challenging outputs (automation bias, Unit 2.2).
END NARRATION

A micro-health product uses an ML model to price cover. Pricing on this pattern would penalise access to care rather than risk.

### SLIDE 08 - Recommended techniques

**On-screen text:** Ask of every use case: Who could be treated unfairly?

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Ask of every use case: Who could be treated unfairly? Can we explain the outcome to the customer? What happens if it fails? Who is the vendor and what do they do with our data? How will we know if it drifts?
END NARRATION
