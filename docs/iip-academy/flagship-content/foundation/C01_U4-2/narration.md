### SLIDE 02 - Insurance supervision and organisational accountability

**On-screen text:** The International Association of Insurance Supervisors published an application paper on AI supervision in July 2025.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
The International Association of Insurance Supervisors published an application paper on AI supervision in July 2025. It addresses proportionality, governance and accountability, robustness and security, transparency and explainability, and fairness and redress. Treat it as supervisory guidance, not an accreditation or an automatic finding that a product complies with Ugandan law. In a review, identify which risk is relevant, who owns it and what evidence supports the control.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 03 - A practical risk-management workflow

**On-screen text:** The NIST AI Risk Management Framework offers four connected functions.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework offers four connected functions. Govern establishes ownership and policy. Map describes context, affected people and risks. Measure tests and tracks relevant performance and risks. Manage prioritises and responds to the findings. An organisation can use these functions to structure its own review. A completed form is not proof that a control works; retain test results and the decision made from them.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 04 - Applicable law is a separate question

**On-screen text:** An insurer using AI must identify the laws, supervisory requirements and contractual obligations that apply to its actual activities and information processing.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
An insurer using AI must identify the laws, supervisory requirements and contractual obligations that apply to its actual activities and information processing. For Uganda, consult the current official data-protection instruments, insurance legislation and regulator material with the relevant legal or compliance owner. Do not infer that an activity is unregulated because there is no familiar AI-specific label. Avoid relying on an unverified section number, a proposal or a remembered regulatory deadline.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 05 - International links require an applicability review

**On-screen text:** A foreign framework can be a useful reference without being the law that governs a particular activity.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
A foreign framework can be a useful reference without being the law that governs a particular activity. The EU AI Act has its own territorial scope, system categories, exceptions and implementation dates. A firm with international operations needs a current applicability review rather than a blanket statement that every insurance AI tool is high-risk or that a customer location alone decides the answer. Use the current official text and record the reviewer, date and reasoning. This unit does not provide a definitive legal opinion or teach a fixed commencement date.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 06 - Work through a governance review

**On-screen text:** Consider a fictional insurer preparing claim summaries.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Consider a fictional insurer preparing claim summaries. The process owner describes the documents, users and intended result. The information-governance owner checks permitted processing and vendor terms. The reviewer tests the summaries against source documents and examines missing information, language errors and unsupported coverage statements. The manager decides whether the evidence justifies a bounded pilot and records conditions. A legal question that remains unresolved is assigned, not filled with an AI-generated answer.
END NARRATION

### SLIDE 07 - Apply the distinction

**On-screen text:** Prepare a small table with four columns: source or framework, its role in your review, the evidence you need and the accountable owner.

**Visual:** Editable teaching text and an insurance example; no invented system screenshot.

NARRATION:
Prepare a small table with four columns: source or framework, its role in your review, the evidence you need and the accountable owner. Separate a voluntary framework, international supervisory guidance, applicable law and the organisation's own policy. Name one question you cannot resolve from the supplied information and write the precise request you would send to the relevant owner. This makes the uncertainty actionable without pretending that the review itself grants approval.
END NARRATION
