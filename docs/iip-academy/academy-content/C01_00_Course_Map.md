# Course 1 — AI & Agentic AI for Insurance

## Course map: modules, units and learning outcomes

### Course outcome

On completing this course, an insurance professional working in Uganda — a claims officer, underwriter, broker, bancassurance relationship manager or customer service officer — will be able to identify where AI and agentic AI can add value in their own work, use generative AI tools safely and productively, verify AI output before relying on it, protect personal data in line with the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019, and assess an AI use case against recognised governance principles so that it can be proposed responsibly to their organisation.

### Target learners and entry level

The course assumes working knowledge of insurance operations but no technical AI background. Learners need a smartphone or computer and access to at least one general-purpose AI assistant for guided practice.

### Structure at a glance

The course has four modules and twelve units. Each unit has an 8–12 minute video and a 10-question quiz; each module closes with a 15-question assessment; the course ends with an applied task. Total video time is approximately 96–144 minutes.

### Assessment blueprint

Unit quizzes contain ten questions each: roughly three test understanding of concepts, four test application to a scenario, and three test judgment (choosing the best course of action or spotting an error). Module assessments contain fifteen questions drawn across all units in the module, weighted towards integrated scenarios. Suggested pass marks are 70% for unit quizzes (unlimited attempts, questions randomised from the bank), 70% for module assessments (maximum three attempts, scenario variants rotated), and 60/100 on the final applied task under the rubric provided. The certificate is issued only when every unit quiz, every module assessment and the final task are passed.

### Video template used for every unit

Each video opens with a practical problem and the learning promise (about 1 minute), explains the concept (about 3 minutes), demonstrates it with an insurance example (about 3 minutes), covers practical application and common mistakes (2–3 minutes), and closes with a summary and transition to the quiz (about 1 minute).

### Dependencies before recording

Section numbers for the Insurance Act 2017 and DPPA 2019 must be confirmed against primary texts; Ugandan scenarios should be validated through practitioner interviews; demonstration tools must be selected and their data terms documented; and the Ministry of ICT AI strategy status should be rechecked before Unit 4.2 is recorded. These are tracked in the master register.
