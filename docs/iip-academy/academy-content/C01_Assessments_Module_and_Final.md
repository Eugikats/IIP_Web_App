## Module 1 Assessment (15 questions)

1. **A system reads incoming claim emails and copies details into the core system by mimicking staff clicks. It is best classified as:** A) RPA B) Predictive ML C) Agentic AI D) Generative AI — **A.** It repeats scripted actions and does not learn or plan.
2. **Which tool most clearly qualifies as agentic?** A) A rules table for referrals B) A fraud score C) A system that receives a claim notification, checks the policy, requests missing documents and opens a file D) A spell-checker — **C.**
3. **An AI tool quotes "Section 112 of the Insurance Act" for a claims timeline. Before using it you should:** A) Trust it B) Open the Act and confirm the section says this C) Ask the tool again D) Remove the reference silently — **B.**
4. **A GenAI tool has no browsing and its training ended last year. Asked about an IRA circular issued last month, it will most likely:** A) Report it accurately B) Lack the information or fabricate a plausible answer C) Refuse all questions D) Contact the IRA — **B.**
5. **Which statement about Uganda's 2025 insurance performance is correct?** A) GWP fell B) GWP exceeded UGX 2 trillion, driven strongly by life business C) Life premiums declined D) Claims paid exceeded premiums — **B.**
6. **Gross claims paid were 46.2% of GWP in 2025. A colleague says this means insurers made a 53.8% profit. Why is this wrong?** A) It is correct B) Expenses, commissions, reserves, reinsurance and investment results are not included C) Claims were higher D) GWP was lower — **B.**
7. **Which first AI project best fits a Ugandan bancassurance team?** A) Autonomous credit-life underwriting without review B) Compliance-reviewed plain-language product explanations drafted from approved product sheets C) Replacing relationship managers D) An agent that changes policy terms — **B.**
8. **The same question asked twice gives two different answers. The professional takeaway is:** A) The tool is broken B) Outputs vary, so important answers must be checked against sources C) Use only the first answer D) Use only the second — **B.**
9. **Which task is AI most reliable at without additional tools?** A) Exact premium arithmetic B) Restructuring a provided text into a clearer format C) Citing current regulations D) Reporting live payment status — **B.**
10. **A medical insurer receives mostly photographed invoices. What should it test before adopting document-extraction AI?** A) Nothing B) Accuracy on its own real photographed invoices C) The vendor's logo D) Only typed invoices — **B.**
11. **A vendor claims its "AI" learns from every claim. On review, referral rules are updated manually each quarter. What should the buyer conclude?** A) It is ML B) It is rules-based; risks and maintenance needs differ from ML C) It is agentic D) It is GenAI — **B.**
12. **Which pairing of category and characteristic limitation is correct?** A) Rules — fabricate content B) ML — inherits bias from historical data C) GenAI — cannot produce text D) Agents — cannot use tools — **B.**
13. **Why has the IRA urged automation of claims processing?** A) To reduce premiums B) To support timely settlement, which is a legal and customer-trust priority C) To reduce the number of insurers D) To replace brokers — **B.**
14. **A customer-facing Luganda explanation was produced by AI. The minimum safeguard is:** A) None B) Review by a fluent speaker against the English original C) Back-translation by the same tool D) Shortening it — **B.**
15. **Which statement best summarises current insurer practice with agents in 2026?** A) Agents make most final claims decisions B) Agents mainly summarise, route and accelerate work while humans keep material decisions C) Agents are banned D) Agents are used only in marketing — **B.**

## Module 2 Assessment (15 questions)

1. **Identify the missing RCTCF element: "You are a claims specialist. Write a message to a customer whose claim is delayed. Keep it under 100 words. Use WhatsApp style."** A) Role B) Context — the reason for delay and documents outstanding C) Format D) Constraints — **B.**
2. **Which instruction best prevents invented details in a valuation report summary?** A) "Be thorough" B) "Use only the text provided; write 'not stated' if absent" C) "Act as an expert" D) "Add helpful assumptions" — **B.**
3. **An AI draft says, "Your claim will be paid by Friday." No decision has been made. Which 3C element fails?** A) Correct B) Complete C) Compliant D) None — **C.** It makes an unauthorised promise.
4. **The AI draft omits the policy excess from a settlement explanation. Which 3C element fails?** A) Correct B) Complete C) Format D) Compliant — **B.**
5. **Which approach to checking an AI-calculated refund is acceptable?** A) Ask the AI to double-check B) Recalculate independently from the policy and dates C) Round to the nearest thousand D) Trust it if formatted well — **B.**
6. **A claims officer pastes a patient's name, national ID and full medical report into a free public AI tool. Which is the main problem?** A) Poor formatting B) Unnecessary disclosure of personal and health data to an unapproved processor, possibly outside Uganda C) The report is too long D) No problem — **B.**
7. **Which is the best way to get AI help on a sensitive medical summary?** A) Use an approved tool with identifiers removed and only needed sections included B) Use a personal account C) Share via WhatsApp D) Avoid summarising — **A.**
8. **Which body must data controllers and processors register with in Uganda?** A) IRA B) PDPO C) URA D) UCC — **B.**
9. **Automation bias is most likely when:** A) Staff have time and a checklist B) Staff are under time pressure and the output looks polished C) Outputs are rough drafts D) AI is not used — **B.**
10. **A team wants a shared library of prompts. The best governance is:** A) Anyone edits anytime B) Tested, approved and versioned templates with an owner C) No library D) Vendor controls it — **B.**
11. **Using claims data for AI-targeted marketing without a lawful basis breaches:** A) Purpose limitation B) Indemnity C) Contribution D) Average — **A.**
12. **A strong prompt for a corporate client's medical-cover summary would include:** A) Only "summarise this" B) The audience (HR staff), the requirement to list waiting periods and exclusions, a length limit, and "use only the schedule provided" C) The client's staff ID numbers D) A request to invent examples — **B.**
13. **A depth-of-review decision for AI outputs should be based on:** A) Length of the output B) The stakes and who will rely on the output C) The tool's price D) The time of day — **B.**
14. **Deleting a chat in a public AI tool guarantees the provider no longer holds the data. True or false?** A) True B) False — it depends on the provider's terms — **B.**
15. **Which record best supports accountability for AI-assisted letters?** A) None B) That the letter was AI-assisted, and the name of the person who verified it C) The prompt only D) The AI vendor's name only — **B.**

14. **An agent processes medical claims. Which additional obligation is heightened?** A) Motor regulations B) Protection of health-related special personal data under the DPPA C) Marine law D) None — **B.**
15. **Uganda's stated principles for AI governance include:** A) Transparency, accountability and human oversight B) Full autonomy C) Vendor self-regulation D) Secrecy — **A.** (S12)

## Final Applied Course Task — AI Use-Case Proposal and Governance Review

### Brief

Choose one real process in your own function (claims, underwriting, distribution/bancassurance, broking, customer service, compliance or finance) where AI or agentic AI could help. Prepare a proposal your manager or risk committee could act on. Use anonymised or illustrative data only — do not include any real customer personal data.

### Deliverables

A completed AI Use-Case Assessment Canvas (all ten boxes), and a written proposal of 1,500–2,000 words covering: the problem and baseline; the proposed solution and its AI category; a decision-rights matrix with autonomy levels per action; a data protection analysis under the DPPA 2019; a risk-and-control table; a mapping to the IAIS five topics and NIST four functions; a pilot plan with success metrics, owner and review date; and two example prompts or agent instructions the solution would use, each with the verification step that would follow.

### Marking rubric (100 marks; pass = 60)

| Criterion | Marks | Distinction-level performance |
|-----------|-------|-------------------------------|
| Problem definition and baseline | 10 | Specific, quantified, clearly linked to business or customer outcomes |
| Solution design and AI classification | 10 | Correct category; realistic about Ugandan data, documents and connectivity |
| Decision rights and human oversight | 15 | Per-action autonomy levels; meaningful escalation triggers; audit and stop mechanism |
| Data protection analysis | 15 | Correct application of DPPA principles, minimisation, cross-border processing and automated decision rights |
| Risk identification and controls | 15 | Covers fairness, explainability, security, third-party, conduct and over-reliance with matched controls |
| Governance mapping | 10 | Accurate use of IAIS topics and NIST functions, not merely listed |
| Pilot plan and metrics | 10 | Scoped, time-bound, measurable, with owner and review date |
| Prompts/instructions and verification | 10 | Structured (RCTCF), grounded, with an appropriate 3C verification step |
| Professional quality | 5 | Clear, concise, ready for a manager |

### Integrity measures

Each learner's process, organisation type and baseline differ, which makes copied or generic AI-written submissions easy to spot. Markers check for consistency between the canvas and the narrative, specific local detail, and realistic numbers. Submissions may be followed by a short oral check (10 minutes) at the marker's discretion.

### Competency verification and certificate

The certificate is issued when all twelve unit quizzes, all four module assessments and the final task have been passed.
