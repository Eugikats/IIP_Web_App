# Claim document fraud review use cases

## Purpose and decision boundary

This is a test and product-requirements inventory for document-based claim-fraud review in IIP. A document should not, by itself, cause the system to label a claimant or claim fraudulent. The system should identify a specific inconsistency, authenticity concern, duplicate, unsupported amount, or cross-claim pattern; attach the source evidence; explain what needs checking; and route it to an authorized human. A missing document is a completeness issue, not evidence of fraud. A discrepancy can also be an innocent clerical error, legitimate correction, translation/OCR error, emergency exception, or insurer-specific process variation.

The cases below combine fraud types publicly reported by Uganda's Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA) with product-specific document checks inferred from insurer claim checklists. They are a broad practical coverage inventory, not a claim that every scenario is common or that every insurer asks for the same documents. IRA's 2024 market report lists reported cases such as motor claims, forged documents/fake policies, fire, cash-in-transit, death/life, property, third-party, medical, goods-in-transit, false information and premium suppression. IRA's 2021 annual report describes motor fraud investigations involving forged claim documents and inflated garage bills. ([IRA 2024 Annual Insurance Market Report](https://ira.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2024-Insurance-Industry-market-report.pdf); [IRA Annual Report 2021](https://www.ira.go.ug/cp/uploads/N_Web_IRA_Annual%20report%202021%20%281%29.pdf))

## Cross-product checks for every submitted claim pack

| Use case | Compare these submitted records | Flag for human review when |
|---|---|---|
| Forged, altered or internally inconsistent evidence | Claim form; ID; police/medical/fire report; invoice/receipt; assessor report; original versus resubmitted file | Names, dates, totals, registration/serial numbers, signatures, stamps, page sequences or issuing authority details conflict; a document appears materially edited; a verification response says it is invalid. A visual anomaly alone is only a low-confidence review reason. |
| Duplicate evidence or duplicate billing | Current and historical claims; invoices, photos, medical bills, repair estimates and discharge records | The same file hash, image, invoice number, line items, damage pattern, service date or receipt is submitted more than once for the same or different loss. Near-duplicate detection must show the matched files and distinguish a legitimate copy from a second payment request. |
| Policy or coverage mismatch | Policy schedule, wording, endorsements, proposal/declaration, premium ledger and claim evidence | Loss date precedes inception or follows expiry; relevant asset/person/location is not the scheduled one; a claimed benefit or extension is not selected; an endorsement conflicts with the schedule; the insured value/limit/excess differs. Flag the exact source lines and allow for renewal, endorsement, timezone and data-entry corrections. |
| Loss chronology or cause conflict | Claim form; first notice; police/fire/medical report; repair or treatment dates; policy inception; prior inspection | The reported event date, notice date, discovery date, treatment/repair start or damage condition cannot be reconciled across records, or a document suggests damage/exposure predates cover. Do not infer intent from late notice or a timeline gap alone. |
| Ownership, identity, authority or insurable-interest conflict | Policy schedule; ID; logbook/title/asset register; beneficiary/authority documents; invoices | Claimant, insured, owner, beneficiary, borrower, employer or asset identity does not match, or authority to claim is unclear. Route to verify; allow ownership transfer, trustee/estate authority and spelling variations. |
| Inflated, unsupported or out-of-scope amount | Claim estimate, invoices/receipts, assessor report, policy limits, agreed tariff, proof of payment | Quantity, rate, tax, repair line, treatment, replacement value or claimed period is unsupported, duplicated, outside the selected cover/limit, or inconsistent with independent assessment/tariff. An amount above a benchmark is a prompt to verify, not a fraud finding. |
| Repeated linked-party or claim pattern | Structured claim history and entity links for claimant, insured, phone, vehicle/property, provider, garage, bank account, intermediary, address and witnesses | A configurable rule finds an unusual cluster, frequency, shared identifier, repeated provider/garage, repeated incident participants, or multiple claims with the same evidence. Show why entities were linked and dates/claims; shared identifiers can be legitimate. Documents alone cannot establish a “four claims in three days” pattern without a reliable claims history. |
| External verification failure | Issuer/provider/police/registry/repairer or approved verification response, with consent/authority and audit trail | An authorized verification channel says a policy, report, invoice, identity or service record is not valid, or it cannot verify a key fact. “No response” or unavailable registry is not a negative match. |

## Product and loss-type scenarios

| Product / loss type | Documents or records to submit and compare | Fraud-risk use cases to test (flag the specific evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| Motor: own-damage accident | Claim form and driver statement; driving permit; logbook/vehicle record; police report/sketch; incident and damage photos; assessor report; garage estimates; final repair invoice; towing receipt | Reported vehicle/driver differs across schedule, police report and logbook; damage in photos is inconsistent with the described impact; pre-existing damage appears in inspection records; repair parts/labour are duplicated, inflated or unrelated; same damage/photo/invoice appears in an earlier claim; multiple parties, vehicles or witnesses recur across claims. |
| Motor: theft or attempted theft | Claim form/statement; police report and investigation updates; logbook/proof of ownership; purchase/transfer record; keys/alarm/telematics evidence where available; assessor/forensic report; recovery/auction records | Vehicle identity, ownership or theft date conflicts; the vehicle was sold/transferred or recovered before the stated loss; the same vehicle has prior loss/claim evidence; a policy or premium record does not support the claimed cover period; submitted keys/ownership records or police material conflict. Do not treat failure to recover a vehicle as fraud. |
| Motor: fire | Claim form; vehicle schedule/ownership; police or fire-service report where available; scene/photos; assessor/electrical/mechanical report; repair or salvage valuation; prior inspection | Cause, location or date conflicts across reports; burn pattern or prior mechanical/repair evidence conflicts with the account; salvage value, total-loss calculation or repair items are duplicated/inflated; vehicle ID or cover differs. AI image observations must be presented as uncertain and reviewed by an assessor. |
| Motor: third-party property injury/death | Third-party demand/claim; claimant ID and authority; vehicle/driver records; police report and statements; damage photos; repair quotes/invoices; medical notes/bills; death certificate/postmortem and beneficiary/estate authority where relevant | Third-party vehicle/person/occupancy is unsupported or conflicts with the police report; repair items predate or exceed incident damage; injury dates/diagnosis/bills do not align with the event; duplicate demand or medical/repair bills appear in another claim; the same parties/vehicle/witnesses recur in unrelated accidents. |
| Property/fire, burglary or theft | Policy schedule and endorsements; pre-loss survey/valuation; title/lease/asset register; inventory; purchase invoices; incident/fire/police report; photos/CCTV; assessor/technical report; repair/replacement quotations and invoices; business interruption accounts if claimed | Claimed property is absent from pre-loss inventory/inspection or ownership evidence; quantity/value differs between inventory, purchase evidence and claim; alleged damage is documented before policy inception; cause conflicts with fire/technical report; the same stock or invoice is claimed twice; replacement/repair cost includes unrelated or pre-existing work; claimed business interruption period or revenue is unsupported by source accounts. |
| Life: death or funeral | Policy and proposal/application; claimant ID/authority; beneficiary nomination; death certificate; medical/physician records; police report/postmortem for accidental/suspicious death where applicable; premium history | Identity, date/place/cause of death, relationship, beneficiary, policy period or benefit differs across records; death is reported before cover or after lapse according to the actual policy ledger; the same life/benefit has a prior claim or duplicate claim; medical evidence conflicts materially with the stated event. Use an authorized civil-record verification channel where available; missing online records alone do not prove a false death. IRA's example list includes claim form, NIRA death certificate, policy document and nominee details, with additional records depending on circumstances. ([IRA Endowment Claims Guidance](https://ira.go.ug/when-to-place-a-claim-on-that-endowment-insurance-policy); [Prudential Uganda Claims](https://www.prudential.ug/claims/)) |
| Life: disability, maturity or surrender | Policy/benefit schedule; claimant ID; medical/disability assessment; employment/earnings evidence if benefit depends on it; survival/age evidence; premium ledger; prior benefit/payment record | Claimed disability type/date or benefit calculation conflicts with medical assessment or policy definition; same loss period or benefit is claimed twice; age, term, premium or payment record does not match; identity/authority differs. Human medical/claims review is required; a model must not make a medical or coverage determination. |
| Health: inpatient/outpatient, dental/optical, maternity or chronic care | Membership/dependant record; claim form; provider invoice with itemized codes; clinical notes/diagnosis; prescriptions; lab/imaging result; referral/preauthorization; admission/discharge record; tariff/benefit schedule/provider contract; remittance and reconciliation | Duplicate member/service/date/invoice; service is not supported by clinical notes or results; code/quantity suggests a higher service than the evidence; charge exceeds selected option or agreed tariff; provider/member dates conflict; admission, discharge, authorization and billing sequence is inconsistent; the same provider repeatedly bills unusual patterns. IRA directs medical claims to be vetted against facility agreement/pricing and further investigated on reasonable suspicion. ([IRA Medical Insurance Claims Directives 2025](https://ira.go.ug/download/circular-medical-insurance-claims-directives/); [AAR Uganda sample medical terms](https://aar-insurance.ug/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Uganda-Individual-plan-30000000.pdf)) |
| Personal accident / group personal accident | Schedule/member roster; claim form; incident/police report where applicable; witness/supervisor statement; medical/disability records; sick leave; payroll/pay slips for income replacement; death/beneficiary records where relevant | Person was not on the covered roster on the event date; injury/event date, work status, disability, sick leave or wage calculation conflicts; same injury/disability period or medical receipt is reused; supervisor and claimant accounts materially conflict. Employer/insurer should verify roster, payroll and medical evidence under policy rules. ([GA Uganda non-motor claims](https://www.gainsuranceltd.com/ug/report-a-claim/non-motor-claims-process/)) |
| Workers’ compensation / employer liability | Employment/appointment record; payroll/pay slips; roster/time sheet; supervisor/incident report; witness statement; police report if relevant; medical records; sick-leave/disability assessment; wage calculation | Employment or wage evidence does not support the claimed period/pay; incident allegedly occurs outside employment but is reported as work-related; duplicate injury or wage-loss periods recur; report, witness, medical and attendance dates conflict; same medical bill is claimed under multiple coverages. ([GA Uganda non-motor claims](https://www.gainsuranceltd.com/ug/report-a-claim/non-motor-claims-process/)) |
| Marine cargo / goods in transit / cash in transit | Claim form and loss statement; commercial invoice; packing list; bill of lading/air waybill; delivery/short-landing certificate; carrier claim notice; customs/dispatch/route records; photos/survey; police report; cash count, custody handover, route, safe/CCTV and bank deposit records for cash-in-transit | Cargo description, quantity, value, route, vessel/vehicle, seals or delivery status differs across documents; short landing is unsupported by carrier/receiver records; same shipment/invoice is claimed more than once; notice or incident chronology conflicts; cash amount is unsupported by count/deposit/custody records; reported robbery narrative conflicts with independent records. UIA’s cargo checklist includes invoice, delivery note, packing list, short-landing certificate, carrier claim, bill of lading/air waybill and damage photos. ([UIA Non-Life FAQs](https://www.uia.co.ug/non-life-faqs)) |
| Agriculture / crop / livestock | Policy/acreage and location schedule; planting/stock register; seed/input invoices; farm maps/GPS; weather or index readings where relevant; crop/animal inspection; veterinary/lab report; mortality/disposal records; sales/yield history; assessor report | Insured location/area/crop/species differs across schedule, farm records and inspection; same parcel/animal/production is claimed twice; claimed loss date/severity conflicts with imagery, weather/index data or independent inspection; stock counts, mortality records, yield or sales are inconsistent. Geospatial/weather mismatches should be review evidence, not automatic rejection; products can use index triggers rather than individual loss proof. |
| Engineering / machinery / electronic equipment | Asset schedule with serial/model; purchase/installation/maintenance records; incident report; alarm/log/system telemetry; technician/root-cause report; photos; repair quotations/invoice; salvage record | Serial number or asset differs; damage appears consistent with wear/maintenance issues while claim describes a sudden event; repair includes pre-existing faults or unrelated parts; the same component/invoice is claimed twice; failure timeline conflicts with system/maintenance logs. Route technical causation to a qualified assessor. |
| Travel | Itinerary/ticket; carrier delay/cancellation or baggage irregularity report; receipts/proof of replacement expense; medical report; police report for theft; visa/booking cancellation evidence; policy schedule | Claimed delay/cancellation does not meet the selected event/period; expense predates the event or is duplicated; baggage item/value is inconsistent with proof of ownership or carrier report; same event is claimed under multiple policies without disclosure where required. Apply the actual policy wording and local product terms. |
| Public/professional/product liability and bonds | Policy/retroactive date and endorsements; contract/tender; claimant demand; notice of circumstance; incident report; pleadings/writ; expert/repair evidence; invoices; correspondence; bond terms and beneficiary demand | Claim falls outside covered activity/period or is a duplicate demand; alleged loss/causation conflicts with contract, incident, expert and repair evidence; claimed amount is unsupported; notice timing or retroactive date differs; same claimant, contract, event or invoice appears in another file. A legal reviewer must interpret liability and policy response. GA's public checklist includes incident/police reports, photos/CCTV, assessment, quotations, purchase proof and third-party demand/pleadings. ([GA Uganda non-motor claims](https://www.gainsuranceltd.com/ug/report-a-claim/non-motor-claims-process/)) |

## Related fraud and integrity cases outside the claimant document pack

Some IRA-reported cases require policy-issuance, intermediary or insurer accounting records rather than claimant evidence. Treat them as separate controls and queues: fake policies/forged certificates require policy/issuer verification; premium suppression requires quote, premium receipt, ledger and remittance reconciliation; unlicensed insurance activity requires licensing/issuer data; intermediary information leakage requires access and audit logs. Do not present these as claimant-document fraud flags. The IRA annual report lists these separately from several claim-related types. ([IRA 2024 Annual Insurance Market Report](https://ira.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2024-Insurance-Industry-market-report.pdf))

## Minimum synthetic QA pack

For each supported product class, create a clean baseline and one-file/one-fact variants so tests can establish why a flag appeared:

1. Clean motor accident pack where schedule, claim form, police report, photos, assessor estimate and invoice agree.
2. Same vehicle with one altered registration/chassis field across schedule, logbook and police report.
3. Duplicate motor repair invoice or reused damage photo across two claims.
4. Theft pack with a single ownership/transfer date conflict and a separate clean theft pack.
5. Property/fire pack with a pre-loss inventory/valuation and one claimed item absent from it.
6. Life death pack with a clean beneficiary chain and a separate identity/date mismatch variant.
7. Health provider pack with a duplicate invoice line, an unsupported billed service, and a tariff variance as three distinct variants.
8. Personal accident/work injury pack with conflicting event, roster, medical and wage-loss dates.
9. Marine/GIT pack with a short-landing quantity inconsistent between packing list, carrier note and delivery record.
10. Cash-in-transit pack with a custody/count/deposit mismatch.
11. Agriculture or engineering pack with a mismatch between scheduled risk and assessor/technical evidence.
12. Third-party pack with repeated linked entities across two files, requiring claims-history data rather than just a single upload.

Every synthetic file must be visibly labeled as test-only, use fabricated identities and contact details, include a private answer key with the exact conflicting fields and evidence location, and avoid language accusing a person of fraud. Keep one intended issue per variant, preserve clean companions, and record whether each flag depends on a document, an authorized external check, or structured history. Score extraction accuracy and appropriate abstention separately from whether the rules flag the test scenario.

## Implementation data needed

Document-only analysis cannot detect repeat-claim frequency, linked providers/garages, prior submissions, premium status, registry validity or cross-insurer patterns without structured, permissioned data sources. The minimum evidence record for each upload should include tenant and claim linkage, immutable file identity/hash, original file and received time, source/channel, document class and extraction version, extracted values with confidence, page/region citation, detected alteration/duplicate signals, and review disposition. External verification and shared insurer databases require explicit connectors, lawful purpose, access controls, provenance, and outage-safe handling. A no-match, unavailable service or absent historical record must not be converted into an adverse inference.

The current IIP work-plan fraud section describes a review score and human advice, not a complete document-fraud coverage guarantee. Before calling these use cases implemented, map each one to its actual rule/vision capability, required source data, tests, tenant permissions and audit evidence. Human reviewers retain all claim outcomes; flags support investigation and do not approve, reject, repudiate or establish criminal intent.
