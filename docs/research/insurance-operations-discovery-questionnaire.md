# Insurance Operations and Business Discovery Study

**Status:** Research instrument draft for pilot and native insurance-domain review
**Language:** English
**Respondent:** Experienced insurance practitioner, manager, executive, specialist, or technology/control stakeholder
**Product disclosure:** Product-neutral. Do not name or pitch any proposed solution during collection.
**Recommended use:** One 90–120 minute interview, two shorter interviews, or an asynchronous depth response followed by a focused interview.

## Purpose, From, To, and use of answers

- **Purpose:** Learn how insurance work, business decisions, controls, technology, and customer outcomes operate in practice, including exceptions and hidden constraints.
- **From:** A practitioner with direct insurance operations, management, commercial, control, technology, partner, or transformation knowledge.
- **To:** The research team responsible for deciding what problems deserve further validation or investment.
- **How answers are used:** To test current beliefs, prioritize follow-up research, identify workflows and outcomes worth validating, expose adoption and risk constraints, and design later pilots. Answers are not evidence of individual performance, market prevalence, legal compliance, product-market fit, or causation.
- **Expected effort:** About 90–120 minutes for a guided full-depth session. For asynchronous completion, spend 45–90 minutes on the sections closest to your work; partial answers are useful.

> **Researcher-only setup:** Sections 1–3 define the method, decision, assumptions, and sampling. Do not include them in a respondent copy. The respondent-facing material starts at section 4.

## 1. Method recommendation

This research should use three connected methods:

1. **Structured Google Form:** gathers comparable organization, workflow, business, technology, and priority signals. It is breadth evidence, not proof of observed behaviour or market prevalence.
2. **Depth interview:** reconstructs recent events, exceptions, rules, handoffs, workarounds, costs, incentives, and decision processes. This is the main method for understanding why work happens as it does.
3. **Optional contextual inquiry or artifact walkthrough:** with explicit consent, ask the participant to walk through one recent redacted case, process map, checklist, queue, report, or screen. Do not capture customer-identifying, medical, payment, credential, or legally privileged information.

The Google Form should not replace interviews. The subject is too operationally complex, institution-specific, and sensitive for a form alone.

The supplied form is deliberately exhaustive because this study prioritizes breadth and insurer-only detail. It is a long-form research intake, not a rapid survey. If completion rate or comparability becomes more important than coverage, pilot results should be used to create a separate 15–25 minute core instrument; do not silently remove questions before learning which sections practitioners can answer reliably.

## 2. Decision and evidence model

The public-evidence coverage scaffold for this instrument is documented in [insurance-discovery-domain-research.md](insurance-discovery-domain-research.md). It identifies what regulators and industry frameworks establish and, separately, which internal workflow and business facts still require practitioner evidence.

### Decision this research informs

Determine which insurance workflows, evidence problems, controls, integrations, user roles, and business outcomes deserve priority in a human-supervised insurance operations platform, and which assumptions should be rejected, narrowed, or deferred.

### Current beliefs to test—not confirm

- Staff reconstruct work across disconnected systems, documents, messages, spreadsheets, and human handoffs.
- Evidence completeness, contradictions, approvals, customer updates, and audit reconstruction create material delay or rework.
- An operations layer beside existing core systems could create value without replacing them.
- Human-reviewed automation may be acceptable when source evidence, limits, correction, accountability, and control are visible.
- A bounded initial workflow can produce measurable operational and commercial value.

### Evidence that would contradict those beliefs

- The problems are rare, low consequence, or already handled well by current tools.
- Most delay is caused by liquidity, external parties, authority policy, litigation, regulation, or capacity that software cannot materially improve.
- Existing core systems can solve the priority problems through configuration or process change alone.
- Staff would need to duplicate work or cannot trust/use another operational surface.
- Buyers have no accountable budget, procurement path, data access, integration capacity, or willingness to change.
- Workflow differences are too institution-specific for a reusable platform without costly customization.
- Automation creates unacceptable privacy, fairness, security, accountability, customer-harm, or regulatory risk.

### Primary learning questions

- What actually happens across the insurance lifecycle, including exceptions and informal work?
- Where do delays, rework, loss, complaints, uncertainty, and control failures arise?
- Which evidence and decisions matter, who owns them, and what makes them trustworthy?
- Which systems, documents, communications, and external actors must stay connected?
- What business outcomes, costs, budgets, adoption barriers, and buying conditions govern change?
- What should not be automated, centralized, exposed, or changed?

## 3. Participant and sampling guidance

Use purposive sampling across distinct responsibilities rather than treating one expert as representative of the market.

Target perspectives:

- Claims officer/handler and claims supervisor or authorized approver.
- Underwriter and underwriting manager.
- Policy administration/servicing and customer service/complaints.
- Distribution, broker/agency, branch, bancassurance, or digital-channel leadership.
- Finance, premium collection, settlement/payment, and operational reporting.
- Risk, fraud investigation, compliance, legal, audit, privacy/data protection.
- CIO/IT, enterprise architecture, security, data, integration, cloud, and support.
- Executive sponsor, COO, head of operations, head of claims, procurement, and finance.
- External contributors where appropriate: broker, assessor/adjuster, garage/provider, TPA, reinsurer, or service partner.

Record role, institution type, insurance lines, decision authority, workflow exposure, and market context. Do not merge different roles into one generic “insurance user.”

## 4. Consent and privacy opening

Thank you for contributing to this study about insurance operations and business practices. We want to understand how work happens today, including what works well, what is difficult, and where practices differ.

Participation is voluntary. You may skip any question, pause, or stop. Please do not share passwords, PINs, access tokens, full payment or bank details, customer names, policy or claim numbers, medical details, national-identification numbers, privileged legal advice, or other information that could identify a person or expose your organization. Use generalized or redacted examples.

Your responses will be treated as confidential research material and used to understand patterns, constraints, and opportunities. They will not be used to evaluate your performance. “I don’t know,” “not applicable,” and partial answers are useful.

Recording requires separate explicit permission. Refusing recording does not prevent participation.

## 5. How to answer

- Prefer a recent real example over a general opinion.
- Describe what happened, what you did next, which artifacts or systems you used, and the outcome.
- Distinguish official process from what normally happens in practice.
- Include exceptions, failed attempts, and workarounds.
- Use approximate ranges if exact amounts, volumes, or timings are confidential.
- For asynchronous completion, answer the most important sections first; partial responses are welcome.

## 6. Moderator and facilitation notes

- Ask one question at a time; do not read all probes mechanically.
- Start with section A. Choose later sections based on the participant’s role and available time.
- Use the participant’s terminology in follow-ups without endorsing an interpretation.
- Ask “What happened next?”, “Can you give a recent example?”, “Was there an exception?”, and “How do you know?”
- Allow silence. Avoid praise, correction, surprise, pitching, or revealing the desired answer.
- Separate observation from inference in notes.
- Stop or redirect if a participant begins sharing customer-identifying, medical, payment, credential, privileged, or otherwise unnecessary sensitive information.
- Never request live access to production systems. Prefer a redacted artifact, staged example, or participant-led description.
- Debrief immediately after each session; do not convert one session into a finding.

Suggested note tags:

`ROLE` `GOAL` `TRIGGER` `STEP` `HANDOFF` `EVIDENCE` `RULE` `EXCEPTION` `DELAY` `REWORK` `WORKAROUND` `COST` `RISK` `HARM` `METRIC` `SYSTEM` `INTEGRATION` `AUTHORITY` `CUSTOMER` `BUYER` `BUDGET` `ADOPTION` `CONTRADICTION` `QUOTE` `FOLLOW-UP`

Post-session debrief:

1. What was directly observed or stated?
2. Which recent events support it?
3. What contradicted the current belief?
4. Which exceptions or minority cases matter?
5. Where may the moderator have influenced the response?
6. What evidence is missing?
7. What should the next participant or method test?

## 7. Optional contextual inquiry plan

Use only with explicit participant and organizational permission.

Observe or walk through:

- One recent redacted work item from intake to the participant’s last action.
- The participant’s queue or method for knowing what requires attention.
- How evidence is located, compared, corrected, and handed off.
- One interruption, exception, or failed integration if it occurs naturally.
- The artifacts used to establish completeness, authority, decision readiness, communication, and closure.

Capture:

`time | actor | observable action | artifact/tool | information used | interruption | workaround | outcome | direct statement | observer interpretation`

Keep the final column separate. Do not capture screenshots, video, documents, customer data, or bystander information without explicit necessity and consent.

## 8. Measurement and analysis plan

### Outcome model

The study should establish:

- **User outcomes:** ability to find current work, assemble evidence, understand status, recover from interruption, complete or route the next action, and explain a decision.
- **Business outcomes:** less active handling, waiting, rework, leakage, complaint effort, and audit reconstruction; improved timeliness, quality, capacity visibility, and adoption.
- **Guardrails:** no weaker human accountability, customer understanding, fairness, privacy, security, accessibility, compliance, or decision quality.

### Candidate baseline metrics to validate with the insurer

| Metric | User outcome | Numerator | Denominator | Window | Direction | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence-ready work items | Work reaches review with required current evidence | Work items meeting institution-defined readiness | Eligible submitted work items | Monthly | Up | Incorrect-readiness rate |
| Active handling time | Staff spend less time reconstructing and re-keying | Sum active handling minutes | Completed eligible work items | Monthly | Down | Error/rework rate |
| Waiting time by cause | Blockers become visible and actionable | Sum waiting duration by reason | Eligible work items | Monthly | Down | No hidden queue transfer |
| Rework rate | Fewer completed steps must be repeated | Work items with repeated/corrected completed step | Eligible work items | Monthly | Down | Corrections remain auditable |
| Customer-update timeliness | Customers receive authorized updates when promised | Updates sent within policy/SLA | Updates due | Monthly | Up | Accuracy and approval compliance |
| Decision-evidence traceability | Reviewers can explain decisions from current sources | Decisions passing evidence-link audit | Audited decisions | Monthly | Up | No exposure of restricted material |

### Form-quality rules

- Treat missingness as data; do not silently impute sensitive or substantive answers.
- Flag duplicate invitation codes or highly similar submissions for review, not automatic deletion.
- Review very short completion estimates, patterned grid responses, contradictions, and empty long answers as quality signals—not proof of bad faith.
- Do not calculate population prevalence from a convenience sample.
- Keep role, institution type, product line, market, and workflow exposure available for analysis without publishing small identifiable subgroups.

## 9. How to collect and read responses

The accompanying Apps Script creates the English Google Form and a linked Google Sheet in the script owner’s Drive.

1. Open `script.google.com` and create a standalone project.
2. Paste the `.gs` file into the editor and save.
3. Select `createInsuranceDiscoveryForm` and click **Run**.
4. Review the requested Google authorization permissions and approve only if expected.
5. Open **Executions** or the execution log to copy the form’s edit URL, respondent URL, and response-sheet URL.
6. In the form description, replace the study-owner/contact, response-access, and retention/deletion placeholders with approved terms.
7. Review every question, option, branch, privacy statement, and organizational term.
8. The script leaves response collection **off**. Preview and test every consent and experience path, pilot with insurance-domain reviewers, then enable **Accepting responses** in the form’s **Responses** tab.
9. Share only the respondent URL. The linked Sheet receives each submission as a row in real time.

The script creates and links the response Sheet automatically. If that link is removed or you want a different workbook, open the form’s **Responses** tab, select the green **Sheets** icon, then create a new spreadsheet or select an existing one. Checkbox answers arrive as comma-separated values in one cell. Grid answers create one response column per row. Long paragraph answers remain separate columns. Preserve the untouched response sheet; analyze from a copy or downstream workbook.

Before collection, also confirm the accountable study owner, participant contact route, authorized response viewers, secure storage location, retention/deletion schedule, withdrawal procedure, incident route, incentive terms if any, and whether organizational permission is required. Because automatic email collection is disabled, define how a participant can identify a response for later withdrawal without requiring unnecessary identity data.

## 10. Limitations note for reports

> This study combines a structured form with optional depth interviews and artifact walkthroughs. Form responses describe reported behaviour, perceptions, and organizational knowledge; they do not directly observe behaviour or prove why an event occurred. Participants were recruited purposively or by convenience and are not statistically representative of all insurers, roles, products, branches, or markets. Response counts describe the study corpus, not population prevalence. Commercial interest, willingness-to-pay, and prioritization responses are directional signals, not demand or revenue forecasts. Associations do not establish causality. Sensitive, unavailable, skipped, or institution-specific information may create missingness and uneven evidence. Findings should retain source and role context, preserve contradictions and minority cases, and be triangulated with redacted workflow observation, administrative baselines, system data, customer evidence, and controlled pilot results before consequential product, policy, staffing, or investment decisions.

## 11. Later synthesis framework—do not populate before collection

Maintain this chain:

`source ID -> atomic observation -> pattern -> interpretation -> insight -> need/opportunity -> implication -> decision`

For each finding, record supporting and disconfirming evidence, roles/contexts represented, source quality, confidence rationale, minority or access-critical cases, and limitations. Counts describe only the analyzed corpus.

Create behavioural archetypes, jobs, journeys, or service-blueprint inputs only after synthesis. Do not invent persona names, demographics, quotes, emotions, or consensus. Every substantive model claim must link to source IDs and an expiry or refresh trigger.
