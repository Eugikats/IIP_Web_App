# IIP market, regulatory, and competitive evidence

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-05
**Geography:** Uganda first; global vendor comparison
**Method:** Primary-source desk research
**Status:** Research input, not an approved market claim, legal opinion, pricing decision, or roadmap change

## Decision use

This evidence pack supports decisions about:

- the size and qualification of IIP's Uganda-first buyer universe;
- practitioner and buyer recruitment;
- deployment, data, outsourcing, and procurement discovery;
- competitive benchmark scope;
- business-case, ROI, pricing, and packaging hypotheses;
- product requirements that must remain provisional until practitioner and buyer validation.

Public evidence can establish the regulated market boundary, aggregate market performance, formal obligations, vendor-published capabilities, and technical marketplace constraints. It cannot establish an insurer's real workflows, claim volumes, unit economics, technology estate, purchase authority, willingness to pilot, implementation effort, or willingness to pay.

## Executive evidence

1. Uganda's insurance sector recorded **UGX 2.024 trillion gross written premium (GWP) in 2025**, up 14.72% from UGX 1.764 trillion in 2024. Life represented 48.31% and non-life 49.49%. Industry assets were UGX 3.459 trillion and claims paid were UGX 934.55 billion.[^ira-2025-performance]
2. The latest detailed full-year market report is 2024. It reports UGX 1.764 trillion GWP, 0.870% insurance penetration, 45.9 million population used for density, UGX 986.477 billion non-life GWP, UGX 702.252 billion life GWP, UGX 69.896 billion HMO GWP, and UGX 1.638 billion microinsurance GWP.[^ira-2024-report]
3. IRA authorised 144 entities for 2026: 19 non-life insurers, 9 life insurers, 3 microinsurers, 1 Takaful insurer, 1 HMO, 2 reinsurers, 5 reinsurance brokers, 49 insurance brokers, 22 bancassurance agents, and 33 loss assessors, adjusters, and risk advisers.[^ira-players-2026]
4. For Release 1's non-life motor-claims focus, **19 authorised non-life insurers are the maximum eligible Uganda account universe by entity type**. This is not reachable market, TAM, SAM, SOM, contracted pipeline, or revenue. The current public record does not identify which 19 have a material motor-comprehensive book, suitable claims volume, Azure readiness, budget, authority, or pilot intent.
5. Uganda does not impose an absolute in-country storage rule in section 19 of the Data Protection and Privacy Act. Processing or storage outside Uganda requires adequate protections at least equivalent to the Act or data-subject consent.[^dppa]
6. Insurer outsourcing is not merely a software purchase. Uganda's licensing and governance rules require governance, written terms, confidentiality protection, access to relevant information, monitoring, termination and dispute provisions, Uganda governing law, and contingency planning; off-shore outsourced activities may also create regulatory inspection implications.[^licensing-governance]
7. The strongest public competitive overlap comes from insurance-native claims platforms and AI layers. Core suites such as Guidewire, Duck Creek, and Sapiens can make a separate IIP layer unnecessary for some buyers; specialists such as Shift, Sprout.ai, and Five Sigma overlap directly with claims intelligence and workflow; hyperscaler and low-code components support in-house substitutes.
8. No reviewed official product page provides comparable public licence pricing. No reliable Uganda-specific competitor adoption, implementation cost, or product-level ROI evidence was found. Pricing and ROI must remain hypotheses.

| Funnel stage | Count at 2026-08-05 | Meaning |

| Buyer-validated accounts | 0 | No completed buyer study is recorded |
| Willing paid-pilot accounts | 0 | No signed or procurement-approved commitment is recorded |

The **currently evidenced reachable initial segment is therefore zero validated accounts**, with a ceiling of 19 candidates before qualification. This is a research-status statement, not a conclusion that no market exists. A named-account register should count each gate separately: relevant motor portfolio; recurrent evidence-review problem; usable baseline/data; acceptable deployment and residency; integration feasibility; economic buyer; procurement path; change capacity; and paid-pilot commitment. Only accounts passing all required gates enter the reachable segment; price-tested reachable revenue is the sum of approved account-level expected contract values, not a percentage of insurance premium.

### Concentration and entry implication

IRA reported that five non-life insurers generated 60% of non-life premium in 2024, while three life insurers generated more than 60% of life premium.[^ira-2024-release] This supports a **concentrated-account recruitment hypothesis**, not a conclusion that the largest firms are the best first customers. Larger firms may have more volume and budget but also stronger incumbent suites, heavier integration, security review, and procurement complexity. Smaller firms may have sharper unmet needs but less implementation capacity. Both propositions require buyer interviews.

### Procurement evidence boundary

No authoritative public source establishes a single procurement process for Uganda's privately operated insurers. Public procurement law should not be projected onto every buyer. Practitioner/buyer research must establish:

- economic buyer, budget owner, technical approver, security/privacy/legal approvers, procurement owner, and final signatory;
- tender/RFP thresholds, vendor prequalification, local-presence requirements, tax/currency terms, payment terms, and contracting entity;
- data-processing agreement, audit rights, subprocessors, support access, service credits, insurance/indemnity, exit, deletion, and portability;
- Azure subscription ownership, CSP status, region policy, identity administration, managed-resource-group access, and cloud cost owner;
- implementation partner requirements, integration certification, change-management capacity, training, support hours, and business-continuity obligations.

## Comparative implications for IIP research

These are hypotheses to test, not approved positioning claims:

1. **Overlay versus core replacement:** IIP may be more attractive where a buyer wants to preserve its core system and add grounded workflow intelligence. This advantage disappears if the incumbent suite already meets the need at acceptable cost and risk.
2. **Evidence and human accountability:** IIP's intended emphasis on current evidence, correction, approval, provenance, and audit could differentiate it. Public vendor pages do not prove competitors lack these capabilities; demonstrations and contract evidence are required.
3. **Customer-subscription deployment:** Azure Managed Application deployment may reduce residency and control objections for Azure-committed buyers. It can also introduce subscription, region, publisher-access, CSP, skills, and cloud-cost barriers.
4. **Uganda workflow/localisation:** No reviewed global vendor page proves Uganda claims, policy, regulatory, language, bandwidth, repairer, payment, or data integration fit. IIP cannot claim local superiority until practitioner validation and implementation proof exist.
5. **Narrow Release 1:** Tractable/CCC may be stronger for image-based physical-damage estimation; core suites may be stronger for full claim administration; Sprout/Shift may be stronger in mature AI claims automation. IIP's first wedge must be defined against a specific unmet job, not the broad phrase "AI for claims."

### Blue-ocean strategy hypotheses

This is an **Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create test**, not approved differentiation:

| Action | Hypothesis for Release 1 | Test against |
|---|---|---|
| Eliminate | Autonomous-decision messaging, broad suite claims, and feature bundles outside the evidence-review job | Buyer trust, scope comprehension, and reasons for rejection |
| Reduce | Core-system replacement burden, deep write-back, opaque AI, and dashboard-only reporting | Existing suite capability, integration cost, review burden, and switching risk |
| Raise | Source visibility, correction/recovery, human authority, workflow lineage, low-bandwidth continuity, and audit evidence | Representative tasks, security/compliance review, and full cost-to-serve |
| Create | A bounded insurer-controlled evidence-review layer with explicit “known/missing/conflicting/stale” state and measurable handoff preparation | Manual workflow, core-suite configuration, low-code/in-house build, and direct AI claims specialists |

The comparison factors for practitioner and buyer testing are: job coverage; evidence provenance; human-control clarity; correction and recovery; integration/disruption; deployment/data control; low-bandwidth operation; insurer configuration; implementation time; cost predictability; support burden; and measured outcome. IIP should not score itself. Each target institution must compare the current workflow, incumbent configuration, in-house/low-code option, relevant direct vendor, and IIP prototype using observed evidence and buyer constraints.

- Shadow systems, paper/email/WhatsApp steps, duplicate entry, verbal approvals, spreadsheet controls, and evidence loss.
- Human review workload, decision thresholds, correction/return/escalation paths, recourse, and automation boundaries.

### Competitors and alternatives

- Current or shortlisted products at Uganda insurers and the reasons they won, lost, stalled, or were rejected.
- Local implementation partners, product localisation, insurer references, service levels, support hours, and exit experience.
- Comparable total cost: licences, implementation, integration, migration, cloud, support, training, control, and change management.
- Verified accuracy, coverage, false-positive/negative rates, latency, explainability, human override, and audit evidence on representative Uganda data.

## Research limits and update rule

- This is desk research only. No insurer practitioner, buyer, regulator, broker, assessor, policyholder, claimant, implementation partner, or competitor customer was interviewed.
- Regulator releases are strong evidence of aggregate values and formal boundaries, not proof of internal insurer practice, market causation, demand, budget, or product fit.
- Vendor pages are first-party capability claims. Treat ROI, accuracy, automation, implementation-time, customer outcome, and comparative claims as promotional until tested with representative data and customer references.
- Absence of a public page is not proof a capability, customer, local partner, or price does not exist.
- No TAM, SAM, SOM, price, ARR, ROI, payback, or adoption forecast should be approved from this pack alone.
- Recheck market metrics annually, authorised-player counts at each licensing cycle, law/guidance before each procurement, Azure region/service availability at each deployment, and vendor capabilities before each comparison.
