# Insurance professional discovery: public-evidence domain map

**Research date:** 2026-08-04
**Scope:** Uganda-first, product-neutral framing for discovery with an experienced insurance professional; broadly applicable insurance-supervision sources are used where they add lifecycle coverage.
**Boundary:** This is desk research, not a questionnaire, interview guide, survey, or Apps Script. It does not treat any public rule, market statistic, or industry framework as proof of how a particular insurer actually works.

## Executive finding

Public sources establish that discovery should cover the whole insurance operating system, not only claims: product development, distribution and sales, underwriting, policy servicing, customer treatment, claims, complaints, reinsurance, actuarial and finance, governance and controls, regulatory reporting, AML/fraud, privacy, data, technology, outsourcing, resilience, and business performance. Uganda's regulatory perimeter also spans insurers, reinsurers, HMOs, brokers, agents, bancassurance, microinsurance, mobile insurance, loss assessors/adjusters and other intermediaries, so the respondent's exact role and operating model must be established before interpreting any answer.[^act][^regulations]

As of the research date, IRA's latest full annual market report is for 2024; current context should be refreshed from IRA's quarterly reporting catalogue and current authorised-player/compliance publications before fieldwork or legal interpretation.[^annual-reports][^quarterly][^players-2026][^compliance-2026]

The public record can identify these domains and external obligations. It cannot establish the respondent's real sequence of work, decision rights, queues, exceptions, workarounds, internal targets, economics, system constraints, failure patterns, or the reasons staff behave as they do. Those are the central practitioner-learning targets.

## Lifecycle and operating-domain coverage

| Domain that discovery must cover | What authoritative public evidence establishes | What must still be learned from insurer practitioners |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Organisation and regulatory perimeter | Uganda's Insurance Act distinguishes insurers, reinsurers, HMOs, agents, brokers, loss assessors and other intermediaries, while IRA's current regulation catalogue adds bancassurance, mobile insurance, microinsurance, index products, Takaful/Retakaful and specialist regimes.[^act][^regulations] | Exact entity type; group and branch structure; countries; product lines; customer groups; respondent remit; centralised versus local work; regulated and unregulated affiliates; outsourced functions. |
| 2. Strategy, target market and portfolio | IAIS conduct standards place customer interests in business strategy, product design, distribution and product performance; IRA's 2024 market report separates life, non-life, HMO and microinsurance performance and reports material differences by product and distribution channel.[^iais][^market-2024] | Actual target segments; underserved segments; portfolio priorities; profitable versus strategic products; entry/exit decisions; growth constraints; demand signals; competitor substitutes; how strategy reaches frontline targets. |
| 3. Product discovery, design, pricing, approval and review | IAIS expects consumer interests to be considered during product development and distribution and describes pre-launch approval or principles-based governance; IRA's current circular catalogue includes a product-approval checklist, product AML risk-assessment tool and product-approval circular.[^iais][^circulars] | Who originates products; evidence used; research and approval stages; pricing inputs; legal/actuarial/compliance sign-offs; launch criteria; version control; post-launch monitoring; change and withdrawal triggers. |
| 4. Distribution, acquisition, marketing and advice | Uganda recognises multiple distribution forms, including brokers, agents, bancassurance and mobile insurance; IAIS treats intermediaries as a major customer-insurer interface and requires promotions and pre-contract information to be clear, fair and not misleading.[^act][^mobile][^iais] | Channel mix by product/customer; lead sources; quote-to-bind journey; agent/broker/bank responsibilities; commission and incentive effects; referral handoffs; channel conflicts; abandoned applications; customer education and mis-selling controls. |
| 5. Customer onboarding, identity and suitability | FIA guidance requires accountable persons to operate AML programmes, conduct risk-based CDD/KYC, assess customer/product/geographic risk, keep records, report as required and train staff; IAIS likewise frames AML/CFT risk across customers, products, geography and delivery channels.[^fia][^iais] | Actual identity evidence by customer/product; beneficial-owner and PEP handling; screening tools; false positives; incomplete onboarding; approval/escalation paths; refresh frequency; manual checks; drop-off and turnaround time. |
| 6. Underwriting, risk selection, pricing execution and referral | IAIS expects underwriting policy to define product classes, exposure and underwriting limits, emerging-risk treatment, expense controls and the interaction with reinsurance; IRA has also issued class-specific underwriting directives.[^iais][^underwriting-circular] | Real data inputs and risk questions; straight-through versus referred cases; authority limits; overrides; inspections/surveys; pricing discretion; exception reasons; referral queues; turnaround targets; post-bind corrections; where adverse selection or leakage occurs. |
| 7. Policy issuance and administration | IAIS's customer-lifecycle scope includes pre-contract information and post-sale policy servicing; its conduct requirements also require timely, clear contractual information.[^iais] | Quote acceptance, binding and issuance steps; policy schedules and certificates; endorsements; renewals; lapses; cancellations; reinstatements; beneficiary changes; document delivery; duplicate correction; ownership and service targets for each event. |
| 8. Premium billing, collection, receivables, commissions and refunds | IFRS 17 treats premiums, claims, benefits and expenses as insurance-contract cash flows and separates insurance service results from insurance finance results; IRA's market report tracks premiums, acquisition/commission measures and complaints about refunds/unpaid premiums.[^ifrs17][^market-2024] | Payment modes; instalments; cash/mobile/bank reconciliation; credit control; intermediary remittance; unallocated cash; arrears; commission calculation/recovery; refunds; suspense accounts; approval limits; daily cash bottlenecks. |
| 9. Claims notification, validation, assessment, reserving, decision and settlement | IRA's claims guidance maps notification, acknowledgement and document acquisition, site visit/assessor appointment, investigation, settlement offer or repudiation communication, discharge voucher and payment; IAIS expects documented end-to-end claims procedures, claimant status updates, competent staff, dispute handling and insurer accountability for outsourced claims work.[^claims][^iais] | Real claim types and variants; first-notice channels; evidence rules; triage; assignment; reserves; assessor/garage/medical/police handoffs; coverage review; authority levels; fraud referrals; reinsurance notification; payment release; reopenings; salvage/subrogation; failure and delay causes. |
| 10. Customer service, communications, complaints and redress | IAIS requires fair customer treatment, timely complaint handling, customer updates and analysis of recurring complaint risks; IRA maintains a Complaints Bureau framework, and its 2024 report identifies repudiation/unpaid claims, refunds, delayed or disputed settlements, poor communication and benefit computation among recurring complaint subjects.[^iais][^complaints][^market-2024] | Contact channels and ownership; service standards; handoffs from contact centre to operations; vulnerable-customer handling; communication approval; complaint taxonomy; root-cause analysis; escalation and redress; repeat contacts; customer-visible status; what erodes or restores trust. |
| 11. Fraud, abuse, investigation and financial crime | IAIS identifies internal, policyholder, claims and intermediary fraud and expects controls to deter, prevent, detect, report and remedy it; FIA requires AML controls and suspicious-transaction reporting processes.[^iais][^fia] | Actual typologies; signal sources; false-positive cost; investigation ownership; case confidentiality; evidence thresholds; law-enforcement/FIA/IRA handoffs; staff misconduct controls; customer-fairness safeguards; recovery and closure practices. |
| 12. Reinsurance and recoveries | The Insurance Act governs reinsurance placement, and IAIS connects reinsurance strategy to capital, risk appetite, retention, counterparty credit, recoverables and underwriting/claims policy.[^act][^iais] | Treaty/facultative workflows; placement calendars; cession calculation; bordereaux; cash calls; recoverable tracking; disputes; reinsurer data exchange; catastrophe/event aggregation; approval limits; delays affecting settlement or liquidity. |
| 13. Actuarial, reserving, capital, investments and enterprise risk | IAIS risk-management scope includes underwriting/reserving, asset-liability management, investments, liquidity, concentration, operations, conduct and reinsurance; IRA lists a 2025 capital-adequacy and prudential regime and currently requests audited statements, regulatory returns and actuarial reports from insurers/reinsurers.[^iais][^capital-2025][^compliance-2026] | Models and data lineage; assumption ownership; reserve review; capital triggers; stress/scenario routines; asset-liability decisions; investment governance; liquidity forecasting; management actions; report production effort; gaps between actuarial, finance and operations. |
| 14. Finance, accounting, tax and management reporting | IFRS 17 requires recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure of insurance contracts and separates insurance service from insurance finance results; IRA's 2024 report says implementation requires accounting-system, actuarial-model, data and IT capacity.[^ifrs17][^market-2024] | Chart-of-account and subledger flow; close calendar; reconciliations; IFRS 17 architecture; allocation rules; tax/stamp-duty handling; management packs; profitability views; manual journals; audit evidence; late-data causes; which numbers leaders trust. |
| 15. Governance, people, decision rights and control functions | IAIS assigns Board oversight and senior-management implementation for risk and internal controls and identifies risk management, compliance, actuarial and internal audit as control functions; it also requires accountability to remain with the insurer for outsourced material activities.[^iais] | Actual RACI; committees; delegations; segregation of duties; informal influence; staffing and skills; workload; training; performance incentives; quality review; override culture; internal audit follow-up; vendor accountability. |
| 16. Legal, regulatory compliance, reporting and audit | IRA's 2026 insurer/reinsurer compliance circular calls for audited financial statements, returns, actuarial reports and valid PDPO registration, while FIA describes AML policies, risk assessments, compliance reporting, training and independent audit.[^compliance-2026][^fia] | Regulatory calendar and owners; source data and attestations; interpretation issues; evidence packs; examination/audit workflow; breach and remediation tracking; licence renewals; legal review; regulatory correspondence; spreadsheet/manual dependencies. |
| 17. Data governance, privacy, records and customer rights | Uganda's PDPO describes registration, privacy governance, lawful processing, processor contracts, retention and breach-response policies, DPIAs, technical/organisational safeguards, data-subject rights and checks before processing personal data outside Uganda.[^pdpo] | Data inventory and owners; systems of record; lineage; quality defects; duplicate identity; access/role design; consent/legal-basis practice; retention and deletion execution; breach handling; cross-border processors; data-rights fulfilment; sensitive-data workarounds. |
| 18. Technology, integration, digital channels, outsourcing, cyber and resilience | Mobile-insurance rules expressly cover insurers, HMOs, brokers, agents, microinsurers and platform providers; IAIS requires continued oversight of outsourced material work and consideration of business continuity; Uganda's 2026 NISF emphasises leadership accountability, risk assessment, baseline controls and organisational resilience.[^mobile][^iais][^nisf] | Application and spreadsheet landscape; core-system boundaries; APIs/files/email/paper handoffs; integration failures; identity/access; downtime; backup and recovery proof; incident history; cyber controls; vendor SLAs; cloud/on-premise constraints; mobile/offline realities; change and release process. |
| 19. Operating performance, economics and business model | IRA's 2024 report measures premiums, claims, commissions, insurance service results, distribution-channel contribution and market concentration, showing that revenue, claims cost, acquisition economics and channel performance are distinct management lenses.[^market-2024] | Unit economics by product/channel/customer; cost-to-serve; loss/expense ratios; working capital; budget ownership; leakage; productivity; service-versus-cost trade-offs; growth bottlenecks; pricing power; willingness to invest; success metrics and decision cadence. |
| 20. Specialist and inclusive-insurance variants | Uganda has distinct rules or regulatory categories for mobile, microinsurance, index contracts, bancassurance, oil and gas, Takaful/Retakaful and motor third-party business, so one generic lifecycle cannot safely be assumed across all products.[^regulations] | Which variants apply; where the standard workflow changes; customer literacy/access needs; premium size/frequency; partner dependencies; Sharia governance where applicable; index/data triggers; specialist claims/evidence and regulatory approvals. |

## Cross-functional handoffs to make visible

The IAIS framework explicitly connects business strategy, product design, distribution, policy servicing, claims, complaints, risk, capital, control functions, outsourcing and regulatory reporting; it therefore supports treating handoffs as a first-class discovery subject rather than studying departments in isolation.[^iais]

Practitioner evidence is still required for:

- the trigger, sender, receiver, information package, system, authority and expected turnaround at each handoff;
- what causes work to bounce back, wait, duplicate, lose context or require escalation;
- which handoffs differ by product, channel, customer, branch, claim value or risk class;
- how staff know work is complete, who can reopen it, and what audit evidence survives;
- where external actors—customers, brokers, agents, banks, reinsurers, assessors, investigators, repairers, health providers, police, regulators and payment providers—enter and leave the workflow.

## Highest-value practitioner-only evidence

Public sources cannot answer the following organisation-specific matters; discovery should prioritise them:

1. **Recent real cases:** complete walkthroughs of ordinary, difficult, delayed and failed cases, including timestamps, artefacts, systems, people, decisions and rework.
2. **Decision architecture:** who recommends, approves, overrides, escalates and executes; monetary/risk thresholds; informal exceptions; separation-of-duty conflicts.
3. **Actual work versus documented process:** shadow spreadsheets, email/WhatsApp/paper steps, duplicate entry, verbal approvals, queue workarounds and reconciliation routines.
4. **Volumes and variability:** workload by product/channel/event, peak periods, backlog ageing, service times, error/reopen rates and staffing capacity.
5. **Economics:** real acquisition cost, claims leakage, cost-to-serve, payment/recoverable timing, commission effects, cash constraints and product/channel profitability.
6. **Failure demand:** repeat contact, missing documents, incorrect records, referral loops, integration failures, complaint causes, disputed decisions and work created by earlier defects.
7. **Information reality:** authoritative source for each field, provenance, data-quality defects, access limits, document versions, reconciliation ownership and retention practice.
8. **Technology reality:** systems actually used, integration modes, offline/low-bandwidth work, downtime, vendor dependencies, release constraints, security incidents and recovery experience.
9. **Customer reality:** comprehension, trust, channel preference, accessibility, vulnerable-customer needs, abandonment, complaint journeys and differences between purchaser, policyholder, insured, beneficiary and claimant.
10. **People and culture:** incentives, skill gaps, tacit knowledge, workload pressure, handover risk, psychological safety, control bypasses and improvement ideas staff have already tried.
11. **Strategy and constraints:** leadership decisions this research must inform, current beliefs, evidence that would contradict them, investment appetite, regulatory dependencies and non-negotiable boundaries.

## Evidence and interpretation limits

- IRA sources describe the Uganda regulatory and market context; they do not establish one insurer's internal procedure or compliance quality.
- IAIS Insurance Core Principles are a globally accepted supervisory framework, not a substitute for Ugandan law, an insurer's licences, or product-specific rules.[^iais]
- The IRA claims guideline is useful lifecycle evidence but is an older published guideline; current insurer practice, product terms, circulars and law must be checked before treating its indicative timings as current obligations.[^claims]
- The public market report is aggregate and retrospective; it cannot establish respondent-level causation, workflow frequency, internal profitability or customer experience.[^market-2024]
- PDPO, FIA and NITA-U sources establish governance topics, not whether a respondent's organisation satisfies them.[^pdpo][^fia][^nisf]
- This desk research should be treated as a coverage scaffold. Practitioner accounts should be corroborated with real artefacts, observed work, operational data and—where possible—multiple roles.
